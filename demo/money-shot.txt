
=== STAGE 1 - RECONSTRUCT (no session) ==============================
The stripped bundle gives up its whole surface, including routes/APIs a low-priv
user never sees. Note the hidden /api/admin/* endpoints - assembled from minified vars,
so a regex JS-miner misses them; Aperta recovers them from the AST.

$ aperta reconstruct lab/dist/assets --format endpoints
GET /api/admin/users/{param}
GET /api/admin/users
POST /api/login
GET /api/me/orders
GET /api/orders/{param}

=== STAGE 2 - DIFF (low-priv alice vs admin) ========================
Reconstructed surface MINUS what alice can actually reach = the role-gated surface,
isolated. This is the delta no traffic-only tool can see (you never clicked it).

$ aperta diff --base http://localhost:5399 --js lab/dist/assets --session alice.json --session admin.json --format jsonl
{"session":"alice","hidden":"GET /api/admin/users/{param}"}
{"session":"alice","hidden":"GET /api/admin/users"}

=== STAGE 3 - PROVE (attacker alice vs victim bob, sibling oracle) ===

but with a contradiction inside the app: the sibling GET /api/me/orders is the app's
own statement of who owns what. alice pulls an order /api/me/orders attributes to bob,
with own-id=200 and nonexistent=404 controls holding in the same run.

=== STAGE 4 - ORACLES (auto-discover where the BOLA oracle applies) ===
Aperta pairs each object endpoint with its self-scoped sibling list endpoint and
reports applicability honestly. The id format does not matter: the victim's REAL ids
are sourced from the victim's own list, so this is deterministic on uuids/slugs too.

$ aperta oracles --js lab/dist/assets --format text
medium  /api/admin/users/{param}  ->  /api/admin/users
high    /api/orders/{param}  ->  /api/me/orders
sibling-oracle applies to 2/2 object endpoint(s) (100%)
